1. **No wellness network, but 100% of your revenue came through friends.** Cornelia is a friend and Carma is a friend of a friend. "None" means none *in wellness*, not none at all. Your personal network, one step removed, is the only channel that has ever worked for you, so the plan starts there.

4. **20–25 h/week is about 260–320 hours over 90 days.** That's the real budget. Every week spent on the site, the name or the visuals is a week not spent talking to people.

## Premises in the brief I think are wrong

1. **"Renaming costs nothing right now."** It costs no reputation, but it does cost time: three languages, legal pages, the email address in the Impressum, OG images, the one-pager PDFs. Call it 2–4 days. It's worth doing for the right name (Task 3), but give the decision one week at most and keep the rename small.
2. **"The DM should surface a need they hadn't named."** The instinct is right; the timing is wrong. When a stranger with a zero-follower account tells you your website has problems, that's exactly what agency spam looks like. The unnamed need should come out *after* they reply: in the free 15-minute look or the small fix (Q3). The first message has one job, which is to get a reply.
3. **"Translate any need, large or small."** "Any" is both the generic claim and the thing that attracts handyman work. The bridge needs a defined other side: *your website and the tech around your practice*.
4. **"Should I run paid Instagram?"** At stage zero the real question is where your first 20 conversations come from, and none of them will come from ads.
5. **"Do I need a homepage?"** With zero traffic it's the most important page you have, because every bio-link tap and every DM recipient lands there. Its job changes; it doesn't go away.

## Task 1 — The 90-day sequence

**The one number to track: conversations started per week.** A conversation means a real back-and-forth with a potential client or someone who could refer one. Followers and traffic don't count.

**A realistic 90-day target from zero network:** 20+ real conversations in the niche, 3–5 small paid jobs, 2 new site projects, 1 retainer, 2 new testimonials. One project plus a few small fixes plus the conversations would still be a good result. The conversations are what compounds.

### Weeks 1–2: Fix what a sceptic will see, decide, ask
*Rough split: 60% site and decisions, 40% asking.*

**Site fixes (all before any outreach):**
- Remove the citation markers from the legal pages (done).
- Regenerate the Impressum and Datenschutz (see note at the end).
- Change the hero location to Berlin.
- Change "15 years" to "10+".
- Hide Scale and Partnership from the nav and homepage. The URLs stay live.
- Make WhatsApp the main way to contact you.

**Decide the name.** Three days, time-boxed (Task 3). Keep the rename small.

**Pass 2 copy.** Homepage and About, English only.

**Instagram.** Real name, your face, and 3–6 posts so the grid isn't empty when someone checks you out. The goal this early is to look real, not to gain followers.

**Be honest about language on the site** (Q5).

**Ask your three clients**, with specific requests:
- **Mareen:** Carma is a hub. Retreats bring in yoga teachers, facilitators, bodyworkers and venues. Ask for three names ("Who taught or held space at your retreats and might need help with their website?"), and ask whether you can say she suggested you get in touch.
- **Cornelia:** therapists and coaches she knows. Her network may be mostly German-speaking, so ask for the English-speaking ones.
- **Third client:** the testimonial, plus their Berlin/Egypt ceremony network.

**Tell your friends, one by one.** Send a personal message (not a broadcast) to every friend and acquaintance in Berlin, 40–60 people. Ask for an introduction, not for work. This is your one channel with a track record. Pass 2 will write the message; the shape is: *"I've started helping yoga teachers, therapists and retreat hosts in Berlin with their websites and the tech side of their work. Do you know anyone who's been struggling with it? An intro would mean a lot."*

### Weeks 3–6: Show up where they are
*Rough split: 50% finding clients, 50% delivery once work comes in.*

- **In person, about two events a week.** English-speaking classes, workshops, ecstatic dance, sound and cacao events; Latin American community events. Go as a participant, not a vendor. The wellness scene lives in rooms, and people hire someone they've shared a room with. If you practise something, practise it where your clients are.
- **Peer partners, 1–2 coffees a week.** People who serve the same clients but don't build sites: brand designers and photographers who work with wellness people, studio managers, retreat venues. **This is where the Partnership offer earns its place.** A brand designer who works with wellness clients and needs someone to build sites is a repeatable source of clients. Use Partnership as a way in, not as a homepage section.
- **Instagram, engage before you message.** Follow ~40 English- and Spanish-speaking Berlin practitioners and genuinely engage with their posts for 1–2 weeks before sending any DM. Then send at most **5 DMs a day**, each written by hand and mentioning something real. Never bulk-send. New accounts that mass-DM get action-blocked, and the messages read as spam anyway.
- **Online communities.** English-speaking, yoga-teacher and Latin American groups in Berlin. Answer tech questions helpfully and in public. Don't pitch.
- **What you offer in all of these:** a free 15-minute look, then a paid small fix (Q3).

### Weeks 7–10: Deliver, document, adjust
- Every job should produce three things: a testimonial request (use their words, whatever they write), a before/after for Instagram (with their permission), and a referral request.
- Look at which messages got replies and rewrite the ones that didn't.
- **Keep at least 5–8 hours a week for finding clients, even when you're busy.** Feast-then-famine is the most common way solo service businesses stall around month four.

### Weeks 11–13: Decide from evidence
Answer these from your own data:
- Which segment replied and bought: yoga teachers, therapists or retreat hosts? English or Spanish speakers?
- Which door did they come through: new site, retreat launch, or fix/care?
- Did anyone push back on price specifically?

Then narrow the site and the messaging to whatever worked. **Only at that point:** visual identity, a small ads test, and investing in German copy.

### Deliberately NOT doing in these 90 days
- Paid ads.
- Follower growth as a goal, or content calendars beyond ~2 posts a week.
- Blog or SEO content for your own site.
- A visual rebrand (palette, typography).
- Promoting Scale.
- New packages beyond the small fix.
- Rewriting the German and Spanish copy for the new positioning before the English has been tested in real conversations. Fix factual errors only.
- Funnels, automation or lead magnets.
- More than one week on the name.

## Open questions

### Q1 — Do you need a homepage?
**Keep it, but change its job from sales page to credibility page.** With zero traffic, nearly every visitor arrives from a DM, a bio link or an introduction, and they're asking: *who is this, is he real, is it safe?* The page should answer in this order:
1. Face, name, Berlin, and one sentence on what you do.
2. The testimonials.
3. Three doors (the three service entry points below).
4. How to reach you, with WhatsApp first.

**Site structure at launch:**

| Page | Job | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Credibility: you, proof, three doors | Rewrite (Pass 2) |
| New website (Starter) | Door 1: "I don't have a site I'm proud of" | Keep. Cornelia is the proof. |
| Fill your retreat (Growth) | Door 2: launching a retreat or offer | Keep. Carma's sold-out retreat is the proof. Name the nav label after the outcome. |
| Get found / looked after (Care+ → Care) | Door 3: "I have a site; it's quiet or broken" | Keep. Fix the contradiction: the Care headline says *"whoever built it"*, but its FAQ says Care is for existing clients only and Care+ is the way in. |
| Small fix | The low-cost entry point | New (Q3) |
| About | The longer story, including Disney/LATAM | Rewrite (Pass 2) |
| Work | Portfolio | Keep |
| Scale | — | Remove from nav. Reachable by direct link and offered in conversation when it fits. |
| Partnership | Channel to designers | Its own page, linked only from the footer ("For designers"). Technical language is fine there because it's clearly a separate space. |

Using service pages as landing pages: yes, but not as ad targets. In one-to-one conversations, send the page that matches what the person just told you. **Contact:** the multi-step form is fine for someone browsing the site but heavy for someone who came from a DM. Make "just message me on WhatsApp" the main call to action and keep the form as the second option.

### Q2 — Are six packages too many?
Too many to show, not too many to keep. Show three doors and a small fix; hide two.
- **Lead with Starter and Growth.** They're the only packages with proof behind them.
- **Care+ is the door for people who already have a site**, and Care is what follows it (which is already how the FAQ describes it).
- **Hide Scale.** No proof yet, and it's the wrong buyer at stage zero. Bring it up only if a conversation calls for it.
- **Move Partnership off the homepage** and use it as a channel (Weeks 3–6).

### Q3 — The gap at the bottom, and the handyman risk
**Recommended ladder change: add a fixed-price "Small fix" at about €120.** It covers one clearly defined fix, done within a few days, paid upfront. Before it sits a **free 15-minute look**, either a short call or a screen recording.

**Why paid and not free:** free work for strangers attracts people who never pay, and it starts the relationship at €0. A small, fixed, upfront price filters for people who value the work, and it makes the next step smaller. Going from €0 to €890 is a cliff; going from €120 to €890 is a step.

**The handyman risk, head-on.** Small jobs aren't the danger. *Unpriced, open-ended* small jobs are. At 20–25 hours a week, five "quick favours" can eat a whole week and leave nothing behind. Four guardrails:
1. **Keep the positioning bounded:** "your website and the tech around your practice", not "any need".
2. **Fixed price, fixed scope, paid upfront.** Anything bigger gets a quote.
3. **End every small fix with a short written note, "What I noticed":** three observations in plain words. This is where the unnamed need comes out, backed by evidence and after trust exists, instead of in a cold DM.
4. **Credit the fix price toward Starter or Care+** if they go ahead within 30 days. The fix becomes a paid trial of working together.

Cap it at about four small fixes a month. If you're doing more than that, either raise the price or the client should be on Care.

*What would change my mind:* if small fixes consistently lead nowhere (0 conversions out of 6 or more), drop the product and keep only the free look.

**Separate pricing note:** keep the list prices, and treat them as something you're testing. Don't discount; cut scope instead ("a smaller site", not "a cheaper site"). If three or more serious prospects push back on price specifically, rather than on budget in general, revisit. If anyone asks what Carma paid, say honestly that it was an early-client rate.

### Q4 — Disney and LATAM
**Use them as reassurance, never as the headline.** For this audience the logos answer "will this person break my site?", not "is this person impressive?"
- **Home:** no logos and no mention in the hero. One sentence in the section about you, at most.
- **About:** one line, framed around the reader. Direction for Pass 2: *spent 10+ years building software for companies like Disney and LATAM Airlines, and now brings that same care to small practices, without the corporate side.*
- **DMs to strangers:** never. It reads as bragging and as expensive.
- **Partnership page and LinkedIn:** lead with it. Designers read it as competence.

*Sign it's hurting you:* prospects asking whether you're expensive before you've mentioned a price.

### Q5 — Language
**Confirm English first, but for a different reason than the brief gives: it's the language you can sell in.** Target Berlin's international, English-speaking wellness scene by name. It's big, and German agencies serve it poorly.
- **Spanish:** your second niche. Much less competition, and you'd be selling in your own language (assuming it's native). The third client's Berlin/Egypt ceremony network may open this door.
- **German:** keep the DE pages, because your clients' own customers search in German and the Impressum is German. But do no outreach in German. **Have a native speaker proofread the DE copy** before you rely on it: you can't judge it yourself, and German readers will.
- **Add an honest line to the site:** *"I work in English and Spanish — my German is still growing."* It fits the site's existing "Honest about what's possible" voice, and it saves a German-speaking prospect from being disappointed on the first call.
- **Dependency:** any German-speaking client needs to be comfortable working in English. Ask on first contact.

### Q6 — Channels
In order of how much I'd expect each to produce for you:
1. Referrals from your three clients, Mareen first.
2. Introductions through friends (your only proven channel).
3. Peer partners: designers, photographers, venues.
4. Showing up in person in the community.
5. Instagram DMs, engaging first and sending no more than 5 a day.
6. Online communities.

**Not in these 90 days:** paid ads and SEO for your own site. Ads start to make sense once one page has turned conversations into clients without ads, you know which segment buys, and you have four or more testimonials. At that point, run a small test (€200–300) aimed at that page and that segment.

## Task 2 — Positioning

**Critique of "bridge":**
- **It describes you, not them.** "Your bridge to technology" is about the role you play. Clients care about the other side of the bridge: a full retreat, a site that doesn't break, people finding them.
- **"Technology" is the word this audience is afraid of.** Leading with it puts the scary thing in the middle.
- **"I translate tech for non-technical people" is claimed by every IT helper and freelance developer.** A metaphor can't be defensible on its own.

**What makes it defensible is a combination nobody else has:**
1. **Who:** yoga teachers, therapists, coaches and retreat hosts in Berlin, English- and Spanish-speaking.
2. **Proof from exactly that world:** Carma's sold-out retreat and Cornelia's own words.
3. **The relationship:** one person, reachable on WhatsApp, often the same day, in plain language.
4. **Real engineering underneath:** 10+ years at Disney/LATAM level, which almost never comes with points 1–3.

Keep bridge + translate as the motif, and let the niche do the positioning work.

**Positioning statement (internal):**
> For yoga teachers, therapists, coaches and retreat hosts in Berlin who'd rather be with their clients than with their laptop, Fran is the one person who looks after their website and the tech around their practice. They tell him what they need in their own words; he translates it into something that works, and explains it back in plain language. Unlike agencies and freelance developers, he's one person you can message on WhatsApp, with 10+ years of engineering behind him and proof from their own world.

**Two lines, two jobs:**
- **Descriptor.** Sits under the name everywhere and does the job of telling people what you do: *Websites and tech help for yoga teachers, therapists & retreat hosts in Berlin.*
- **Motif line.** Hero, About and Instagram bio; it's what people remember: ***You speak your practice. I speak tech. I'm the bridge between.***

*What would change my mind:* if people in early conversations keep describing you with a different phrase (for example "the tech friend"), switch to their words.

**Testimonial placement** (texts unchanged; this is about placement and emphasis only):
- **Cornelia:** move her from the Starter page to the **homepage**, next to the motif line. Visually emphasise *"stepping in where I lacked technical know-how and ideas"*, because that's the bridge in a client's own words.
- **Mareen:** put her on **Growth** and **About**, emphasising *"he communicates so clearly"*.
- ⚠️ **Mareen's quote does not say "sold out."** That claim has to appear as your own statement next to her quote, clearly separate from it, so it never reads as her words. If you want the result in her voice, ask her whether she'd like to add it herself. Don't edit the quote.

## Task 3 — Name and domain

**Should you rename? Yes, to your own name, not to a bridge name.**

**Why "Base Pages" should go.** It names the product (pages), sounds like a template service or site builder, and says nothing about a person. It suited the old product-first hero, not the new positioning.

**Why your own name wins at stage zero:**
1. **It fits the channel.** Every DM, introduction and Instagram profile shows the account name. "Fran Cortez" with a face reads as a person. "TechBridge Studio" messaging from a zero-follower account reads as agency spam, which is exactly what you're trying not to look like.
2. **Your testimonials already say "Fran."** The brand and the proof match.
3. **It survives repositioning.** If month three shows retreats are your niche, you won't have to rename again.
4. **Lowest trademark risk.** Not zero; still check.
5. **Wellness clients hire people, not agencies.**

*The cost:* the business is harder to sell later and the brand is tied to you. That doesn't matter at stage zero for a solo practice.

**The candidates you floated** (availability and trademarks unverified for all of them):

| Candidate | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `bruecke.digital` | No | A German word your main audience can't spell (ü/ue) or pronounce. It promises German you can't deliver on the first call, and ".digital" sounds like an agency. |
| `bridgedigital.berlin` | No | "Bridge Digital" is a generic agency name, so existing companies probably use it (check DPMA/EUIPO). "Digital" is the tech-agency tone you're moving away from. |
| `techbridge.studio` | No | Puts "tech", the word they're afraid of, first. "TechBridge" is a common name for existing organisations (check). |
| `yourtechbridge.com` | No | Long; the "your" prefix looks like a workaround for a taken domain; same "tech" problem. |

**My proposals:**
1. **Pick: Fran Cortez.** Domain `francortez.com`, with `.de` or `.berlin` as a fallback, and an Instagram handle to match, using the descriptor and motif line above. *Check availability first. Your email being "elfrancortez" suggests the plain handle may already be taken on some platforms.*
2. **If you want a brand that isn't your name: Puente** (Spanish for bridge). It keeps the motif, is easy to say in English and German, ties to your roots and to the Spanish-speaking niche, and doesn't promise German. For example `puente.studio` or `puentestudio.berlin`. It's a very common word, though, so the trademark and confusion risk is **higher**. Check carefully. Choose this one if you plan to hire people or eventually sell the business.

*What would change my mind:* a real plan to grow into a team within 1–2 years. That points to a Puente-style brand.

**Keep the rename small.** New domain; keep `base-pages.com` renewed and redirecting for at least a year; switch the `legal@` address in the Impressum and Datenschutz only once the new email works. Update the header wordmark, page titles and OG images, and the Instagram handle. **Not:** a new logo system, palette or typography.

**Checks before you commit:** domain availability; a trademark search on DPMA and EUIPO in classes 35 and 42; Instagram handle availability; a Google search for businesses with the same name in Berlin.
