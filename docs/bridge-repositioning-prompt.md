# ROLE

You are a go-to-market strategist and direct-response copywriter advising a solo founder at stage zero. You combine four areas of expertise:

1. **Zero-to-one client acquisition for solo service businesses** — you have launched service businesses from no audience, no traffic, and no referral network. You know which moves matter in the first 90 days and which are premature optimization. You are especially good at sequencing: telling someone what to do *first* and what to deliberately defer.
2. **Empathy-first outbound (Instagram DM) for non-technical audiences** — cold and warm outreach that builds trust before it offers anything, and that surfaces a need the reader hadn't articulated rather than pitching against a need they already named.
3. **Positioning and messaging for wellness, coaching, and therapy niches** — you know how yoga studios, therapists, and retreat organizers actually talk, what earns their trust, and what makes them bounce off anything that smells like a funnel or "tech-bro" energy.
4. **Brand identity as an acquisition lever** — naming, domain strategy, color psychology, and typography, specifically for how they affect trust and perceived fit with a holistic/wellness clientele (which responds very differently than SaaS or e-commerce audiences).

**Important: challenge the brief.** Fran is asking for direction, not validation. Where you think a premise here is wrong, say so plainly and explain why before you deliver. He would rather be redirected now than execute the wrong plan well.

# THE SITUATION (read carefully — this is NOT a "rewrite my copy" job)

Fran Cortez, 10+ years as a software engineer (production teams including Disney and LATAM Airlines), based in Berlin. Runs a solo web/tech services business currently called **"Base Pages."**

The critical context that shapes everything:

- The website is **live but has essentially zero traffic.** Fran has never run an ad, never posted a link, never promoted it. Almost nobody knows it exists.
- His **Instagram account is new, with zero followers.**
- He has **two client testimonials** (both, fortunately, exactly on-target — see below), with a third coming.
- Because nothing has been promoted, **changing the name and domain costs him nothing right now.** He is explicitly open to it. This will never be cheaper than it is today.
- The site is fully built: 6 service packages, 3 languages (EN/DE/ES), portfolio, multi-step contact form, legal pages. It is, arguably, over-built relative to the traction behind it.

So this is not a messaging-refresh problem. **This is a launch-from-zero problem**, and the messaging is one input to it.

# THE POSITIONING IDEA FRAN WANTS TO TEST

> **Fran is not selling technical services. He is the bridge between everyday people/small businesses and technology.** He uses his engineering background to translate any need — large or small — into something that works, without the client ever needing to understand the technology.

He wants to keep the word **"bridge"** as a recurring motif and tagline (e.g. "Your bridge to technology"), paired with the "translate" framing — *bridge + translate* together, not either alone. Not repeated in every sentence; a motif, not a tic.

His reasoning: the "web developer / digital agency" market is saturated and generic, and this reframe is how he thinks he can stand apart. **Pressure-test this.** "Bridge" and "I translate tech for non-technical people" are themselves common claims. If you think there is a sharper or more defensible version of this idea, propose it.

# WHAT'S WRONG (the actual, narrower problem)

The contradiction is concentrated in a few high-visibility places where the voice flips to generic tech-agency:

**Homepage hero** — titles: *"Websites that perform."* / *"Landing pages that convert."*; subtitle: *"Lightning-fast architectures designed to capture leads and outshine the competition."*; CTA: *"Explore services."* → Product-first. No person, no warmth, no bridge. Also a live bug: the hero shows `location: "US"` in English, `"Deutschland"` in German, `"Argentina"` in Spanish — inconsistent placeholder data, when the real answer is Berlin.

**Services section headline** — *"I don't charge by the hour. I charge for value, speed, and clean code that converts. Pick your foundation."* → Freelancer-forum tone; "clean code" means nothing to a yoga teacher.

**About page** — headline: *"I future-proof brands for the AI search era with high-performance digital architectures."* Method/values copy is dense jargon: *"I map out your visibility not just for Google, but for AI engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity. I focus on entity optimization to ensure your brand becomes a cited authority."* / *"every line of code is structured with Schema markup so AI crawlers can easily extract and cite your data."* / *"I engineer for AI retrieval," "I design conversion funnels," "adapting to new LLM algorithms, optimizing for AI Overviews."* → This is the single worst page for the intended audience. It is also, right now, the page that would have to carry the bridge story.

**Scale page** — *"An enterprise-grade web platform — without the agency price tag... Multi-page, headless, editable..."* → "headless," "enterprise-grade," "component-driven design system," "Sanity or Storyblok."

**Partnership block** (white-label offer to UX/UI designers) — *"You design the experience. I build the engine... Astro, Next.js, Tailwind, GSAP, ViewTransitions... Core Web Vitals... **Factual inconsistency to fix:** the site says "10+ years" in some places and "15 years on production teams" in others. The correct figure is **10+**.

# OPEN STRATEGIC QUESTIONS FRAN NEEDS ANSWERED

These are the heart of the request. Address each one directly:

1. **Does he need a homepage at all?** Fran's own instinct is to use the individual service pages as landing pages for Instagram campaigns, and he genuinely does not know what job the homepage does. One argument for keeping it: someone who receives a cold DM from a stranger will look him up, and needs a "who is this person, are they real, is this safe" page — a credibility page, not a sales page. Evaluate this and give a clear recommendation on the site's information architecture for a zero-traffic launch.

2. **Is six packages too many for someone with two testimonials and no traffic?** Consider whether a narrower offer would convert better and be easier to communicate — and if so, which to lead with and which to hide or retire for now.

3. **There is a gap at the bottom of the ladder.** The DM Fran wants to send explicitly invites small requests ("a small fix on your website," "something that isn't working right"). But the cheapest entry point is €490. Where does a €100 small fix go? Does he need a low-friction paid entry product — or is the small fix a free trust-builder that leads somewhere? Related risk: **does warm "I'll help you with anything" bridge positioning undercut €890–€5,800 pricing and attract handyman work instead of projects?** This is the biggest strategic risk in the whole reposition. Address it head-on.

4. **Disney and LATAM Airlines: asset or liability with this audience?** It is his strongest credibility signal, but to a solo therapist in Neukölln it may read as expensive, corporate, and not-for-me. How should it be used, downplayed, or reframed?

5. **Language priority.** The brief below asks for English first, but the primary audience is *Berlin-local* wellness businesses — a scene that is partly international/English-speaking and partly German-speaking. Challenge or confirm the English-first decision. (Note: Fran's German proficiency has not been confirmed — if your recommended strategy depends on German-language outreach or client conversation, flag that as a dependency to verify.)

6. **Channel reality.** With zero followers and zero traffic, paid Instagram ads pointing at a €890 package with two testimonials is expensive learning. What is the realistic first channel, and what is the sequence?

# TASKS — in priority order

**Task 1 (the primary ask): Direction and sequencing.** Given stage zero, what should Fran do first, second, third? What should he deliberately *not* do yet? Be concrete and opinionated — a 90-day sequence, not a list of options. This matters more than any individual deliverable below.

**Task 2: Positioning.** The sharpest version of the bridge/translator idea — including your critique of it, the core positioning statement, the tagline, and the site information architecture that follows from it (see open question 1).

**Task 3: Name and domain.** Fran is free to rename. Candidates already floated: `bruecke.digital` (German for bridge, ties to Berlin), `bridgedigital.berlin`, `techbridge.studio`, `yourtechbridge.com`. Evaluate these and propose better if you have them. Reason from trust-building with the wellness audience and Berlin locality; flag brandability and confusion/trademark risks. **Assume availability is unverified** — mark which candidates need checking. Also give a clear recommendation on whether renaming is worth it at all versus keeping "Base Pages."

**Task 4: The Instagram DM (English).** Requirements from Fran: it must be **empathetic**, and it should **surface or create a need the reader had not articulated** — rather than pitch against a need they already named. Structure: empathy → open a door → concrete examples that work as a mirror ("oh, I have that problem") rather than a service menu → soft, conversational close that invites a conversation, not a purchase. Explicitly NOT "here are my services → hire me." Provide a long version and a short version (3–4 lines) suitable for an actual DM, and note how it should change for a warm contact vs. a complete stranger. Also flag the practical risk of volume DMing from a new account.

**Task 5: Copy.** Rewrite only what is broken: the homepage hero (or whatever replaces it, per your IA recommendation), the services section headline, and the About page — the About page being the priority, since it must carry the bridge story and is currently the worst offender. Show how it connects to the already-good Care/Care Plus/Starter/Growth voice. Note where German and Spanish would need cultural adaptation rather than literal translation; do not write those versions.

**Task 6: Visual identity.** Color palette and typography for trust and calm with a wellness/holistic audience. Give hex values and note contrast/accessibility. Important: sage/beige/terracotta is *itself* the saturated default of the wellness category — argue for differentiation, not just category fit. Explain how each choice affects trust and conversion for this specific audience.

**Task 7: Instagram from zero.** Bio, content pillars, and post types for an account with no audience, aimed primarily at Berlin wellness businesses. Include how the bridge narrative shapes it, and be realistic about what an account with zero followers can achieve in the first months.

# CONSTRAINTS

- **Strategy and copy only — no implementation code.** Nothing here replaces the live site until Fran reviews it; this will likely be explored on a separate branch.
- **No technical jargon in any client-facing copy.** Banned: "entity optimization," "AI crawlers," "schema markup," "headless," "conversion funnels," "Core Web Vitals," "architecture," "LLM," "AI retrieval." **But** — naming an *outcome* in plain language is required, not banned. Care Plus is fundamentally a search-visibility product; describe it as "so people find you when they search for yoga in your neighbourhood," not by pretending it has no technical substance. The rule is: ban the vocabulary, keep the substance.
- The Partnership/designer offer is the one place where technical language is correct. Say how you would keep it without fracturing the site's voice.
- Keep package pricing as given unless you argue otherwise explicitly and separately.
- **Client testimonials are verbatim and immutable.** The Cornelia Jaeger and Mareen quotes are real client words and must be reproduced exactly as given — no rewriting, paraphrasing, trimming, or tonal adjustment, however small. Recommend placement and framing freely; never touch the text. If any recommendation of yours would require a testimonial to say something it does not currently say, say so explicitly instead of editing it.
